Have you ever been engrossed or obsessed at a particular topic / study that it also starts to appear in your dreams? For me, I would say it has happened multiple times and has also helped me with solving critical blockers related to that topic. I remember the time, in my 11th standard when I was trying to create a java based ascii game and had been stuck on the implementation for quite some time. I thought about it for days and nights, and I was able to finally crack it when the solution came to me in my dreams. Weird, right? 

Srinivasa Ramanujan, India's greatest mathematician, famously dreamt of complex mathematical formulas and equations, which he claimed were revealed to him by his family's patron goddess, Namagiri Thayar. He frequently described experiencing highly vivid, visual dreams where mathematics would unfold before him. In one of his most well-known accounts, Ramanujan described a dream where he saw a red screen formed by flowing blood. Suddenly, a hand appeared and began writing a series of advanced mathematical results on the screen, particularly involving elliptic integrals. He stated that he would memorize the equations as they were written and then immediately transcribe them into his notebooks the moment he woke up.

I was intrigued by this. Dreams fascinated me, and I would say I have discovered and learnt a lot of new ideas through my dreams at different stages of my life. During my research years, I was heavily vested in the study of abiotic stress resistance in the rice plant. I used to read hundreds of papers in a week, understanding all the biological pathways and interactions of genes and how their network of interactions played a role in its survival to extreme drought, heat and stress. Funnily, I had quite a vivid dream where I was the rice plant itself and I wrote about it immediately as I woke up (I mostly do this for all my dreams). As a plant, I rememver I had a different kind of sense maybe a sixth sense, I had no sense of vision, touch, smell, or the normal human like senses. The only sense I had was about my presence. I only had the feeling that I was present among other plants in a dense field. I could sense the hot temperature and the urge for extracting water from my roots from the soil, but the sense was not exactly how humans would feel it. It was not an uncomfortable feeling due to the heat or a thirst of water. I did not feel trapped in the motionless body, the only feeling I could feel is about acknowledging my own's presence and about survival. I was not able to think, it was an instictive movement for growth, where I could see my roots expanding to more depths of the soil where water could be present, or my leaves drowsing to avoid loss of water from my pores. At moments, I would zone out of the plant and had this blissful feeling where I felt I have figured out life and found answers to all my questions. I could magically see all my research studies, the biological pathways and mechanisms play out in front of me and making so much sense. This information and feeling is extremely blissful, so much so a fear suddenly creeps in. I feel that if I give in to my emotions I might forget everything else, I might lose my sense of existence. I stand there in a dilemma, either to savour this experience for the rest of my infinite life or to come back into reality where I remember my past and my human form. But coming back into reality would mean I forget about all the things I figured out in my blissful state. I would wonder what my real life would look like if I gave into this blissful infinite void. Probably I would go into a perpetual coma or would go mad. This sudden fear immediately pulls me out of the dream. It was a good feeling, I started to appreciate my research even more. Probably the subject doesnt matter, it is all about the depths you explore in any domain.

These dreams started to make me more intrigued by them. I wanted to understand how they function at a scientific level, a subject called oneirology. However, it is a relatively new domain and there has been little and almost no conclusive research that has happened on them. One of my entrepreneural courses at college, gave me a chance to do some research into how the brain works and what part gets activated during different times of the day and during sleep. I was trying to pitch a startup idea based on cognitive based learning for school students. The idea was to understand how our brain functions at different times of the day and accordingly make a study plan of what subjects and topics to study. During that time, I read research papers and got to learn that we had two specific brain lobes focussing on different aspects, the logical/analtical side and the creative side. When we are close to sleep or dreamy state then our creative side peaks, however during the day when we are wide awake then our logical side takes the forefront. Hence it made sense to do maths during the day while do literature or art during early morning or late night. Its easier to explain logic, while its difficult to explain creativity. Creativity is like an altogether different dimension, it is something that needs to be felt, experienced. It cannot be taught, but can be awakened. I think that is what happens when we dream, we reach an elevated stage of our minds and it helps in potentially unlocking knowledge that helps us in the real world.

Whenever we reach to the depths of any knowledge we discover certain truths about life. Another instance about my dream was during my first job when I did intense reading as well as a lot of work in the field of maching learning and artificial intelligence. Here I dreamt of being a neuron. In this parallel universe, each human being is nothing but a neuron. One human being interacts with the other to pass information and update their states. The whole human kind can be compared to a Neural network, the aim of which is to constantly evolving and gaining more knowledge. In a journey of training, a neuron encodes info within itself while paying specific attention to the latest information it receives and discarding any old useless noisy info. Even in our life journey we only keep what is relevant, rest we forget. The key is survival is being relevant, the key to being beyond survival is knowing what's relevant.

An ML system is constantly in epochs bombarded with new info again and again until it is able to discover patterns within it and structure it. The ultimate aim being to increase their intelligence. Dont we do that in our real life as well? We keep learning new things, forget the old ones and our intelligence keeps on increasing. Whenver we retrospect, we are doing some form of meta thinking. In a neural network, this can be thought of as linear layers (or dimensions). The last layer has all the structured information from the previous layers and it could try to retrospect and structure it even further. We often try to understand ourselves, probably that is when we are building one more layer of depth.

Other people are other neurons or are they earlier epochs.

(Since I already know about LSTM and transformers, I'm one dimension above them)

It occured to me:
I am nothing but knowledge. My purpose is to increase myself. Whenever I study anyhting, I find myself in it. Whatever knowledge I have gained in my life are my past. My current self while revisiting those knowledge is evolving itself (it is bucketing them, finding patterns, creating justifications for events). Whatever knkoweldege I gain as time goes on, are a superset of my previous knowledge. The latest that I've learnt is AI and I think it will be able to encode or supersede all my previous knowledge like bioinformatics or logic gates.

Trying to find the meaning of life in the depths of knowledge.
